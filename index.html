Organelle Functions — Must Know

  • Nucleus: contains DNA; controls cell activities
  • Ribosomes: site of protein synthesis (Paper 4 Q1 — protein synthesis location)
  • Mitochondria: site of aerobic respiration; produces ATP energy
  • Cell membrane: partially permeable; controls what enters/leaves

  • Vacuole (plant): contains cell sap; maintains turgidity

Organisation Hierarchy

Cells → Tissues → Organs → Organ Systems → Organism
e.g. Muscle cells → Muscle tissue → Heart (organ) → Circulatory system

Tissue = group of similar cells; Organ = made of several different tissues; Organ system = several organs working together.

Three Types of Transport — Key Distinctions

FeatureDiffusionOsmosisActive Transport
SubstanceAny molecule/ionWater onlyIons/molecules
DirectionHigh → Low conc.High → Low water potentialLow → High conc.
Membrane needed?Not alwaysYes (partially permeable)Yes

⚠️ Active transport goes AGAINST the concentration gradient and REQUIRES ENERGY. This is the key distinguishing feature (Paper 2 Q1).

Osmosis in Plant Cells

  • Turgid cell: water enters by osmosis when outside solution has higher water potential → cell swells, cell wall prevents bursting → turgor pressure
  • Plasmolysis: water leaves by osmosis when outside solution has lower water potential (e.g. concentrated salt solution) → cytoplasm shrinks → membrane pulls away from cell wall
  • Potato experiment: if potato is in concentrated salt solution, it loses mass (water leaves by osmosis). If in pure water, it gains mass.

Root Hair Cell Adaptations

  • Long thin extension increases surface area for absorption
  • Water absorbed by osmosis — soil water has higher water potential than root cell contents
  • Nitrate ions absorbed by active transport — against concentration gradient, requires energy
  • Uses of water in plant: photosynthesis, transport of minerals, maintaining turgor
  • Uses of nitrate ions: make amino acids → proteins for growth

Rate of Diffusion

Depends on: concentration gradient (steeper = faster); temperature (higher = faster); surface area; distance; molecular size (smaller molecules diffuse faster — Paper 2 Q18: ammonia NH₃ has smallest Mr so diffuses fastest).

B4 Enzymes
▼

Key Facts

  • Enzymes are proteins — they are biological catalysts
  • Each enzyme has an active site with a specific shape complementary to its substrate (lock-and-key model)
  • Enzymes are not used up in reactions — they are reusable

Effect of Temperature

  • As temperature rises → faster rate (more kinetic energy, more frequent collisions)
  • At optimum temperature → fastest rate
  • Above optimum → rate decreases rapidly → enzyme becomes denatured
  • Denaturation = irreversible change to active site shape → substrate can no longer bind
  • Do NOT say enzyme is "destroyed" or "killed" — say denatured

Effect of pH

  • Each enzyme has an optimum pH
  • pH too high or low → active site changes shape → enzyme denatured (or less active)
  • α-amylase (pancreatic): optimum ~pH 7; denatured at pH 9
  • β-amylase (bacterial): active over wider pH range as bacteria encounter varied environments

Amylase (Paper 4 Q3 + Paper 2 Q10)

  • Amylase breaks down starch into maltose (sugar)
  • Seeds contain amylase to convert stored starch into glucose for energy during germination
  • Starch → amylase → maltose → glucose → respiration → ATP for growth
  • Test: iodine solution turns blue-black if starch present; remains orange-brown when all starch broken down
Exam command: "Explain the activity of α-amylase at pH 9"
At pH 9, the active site of α-amylase changes shape (is denatured). The substrate can no longer bind to the active site, so the enzyme cannot catalyse the reaction. Activity is very low/zero.

Catalase (Paper 6 Q1)

  • Found in celery cells; breaks down hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen
  • 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂
  • Foam height increases as oxygen released → rate decreases and levels off as substrate used up
  • Rate fastest at start; slows as H₂O₂ concentration decreases

Cooling curve for pure water — G = gas cooling, flat sections = changes of state, F = freezing

Physical vs Chemical Changes (Paper 2 Q27)

  • Physical change: reversible, no new substance made (e.g. melting, evaporation, dissolving)
  • Chemical change: new substance(s) made (e.g. combustion, decomposition, neutralisation)
  • Paper 2 Q27: Liquid water → steam (physical); CH₄(l) → CH₄(g) (physical) → answer C (2 and 4)

Thermal Decomposition of Metal Carbonates (Paper 6 Q4)

Rate = 100 ÷ time (s)  → units: per 100 s

P1 Motion: Speed, Velocity & Acceleration
▼
speed (m/s) = distance (m) ÷ time (s)  |  v = u + at  |  a = Δv ÷ Δt

Speed vs Velocity

  • Speed: scalar — magnitude only (e.g. 10 m/s)
  • Velocity: vector — magnitude AND direction (e.g. 10 m/s north)
  • Velocity = speed in a stated direction

Speed-Time Graphs

Speed-time graph: gradient = acceleration; area = distance
  • Gradient of speed-time graph = acceleration
  • Area under speed-time graph = distance travelled
  • Horizontal line = constant speed (zero acceleration)
  • Steeper gradient = greater acceleration

Worked Examples from Paper 4

Acceleration Calculations

a = (v - u) / t  |  F = ma  |  Car crash: 35 m/s² ÷ 9.8 m/s² = ratio 3.57

Vectors: velocity ✓, acceleration ✓, weight ✓, gravitational field strength ✓. NOT vectors: mass, speed, distance, energy, temperature (Paper 4 Q7b, Paper 2 Q28).

Pendulum (Paper 6 Q5)

  • Measure 20 oscillations at once → reduces % timing error / effect of reaction time
  • Repeat and average → improves reliability
  • Longer pendulum → longer period T → easier to time accurately → reduces % error
  • g = 0.395 × L / T² (where L in cm, T in s → g in m/s²)
  • Compare with expected value: within 10% of 9.8 m/s² → agree within experimental accuracy
P2 Forces & Newton's Laws
▼

Newton's Laws

1st Law: An object remains at rest or constant velocity UNLESS acted on by a resultant force.
2nd Law: F = ma (resultant force = mass × acceleration)
3rd Law: For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Resultant Force (Paper 2 Q37, Q39; Paper 4 Q9)

  • If all forces balanced (resultant = 0) → constant velocity or stationary (Newton's 1st)
  • Paper 2 Q37: object moving at constant speed → equal and opposite forces → answer C (2N left, 2N right)
  • Paper 2 Q39: large P left, small Q right → resultant = P – Q (acting leftward)
  • Paper 4 Q9 (angler fish): Force A = weight; Force A balanced by upthrust; Force B (thrust) balanced by drag; no resultant → constant speed

P3 Hooke's Law & Springs
▼
F = kx  |  k = spring constant (N/m or N/cm)  |  x = extension or compression
Extension–load graph: linear region = Hooke's Law obeyed; k = gradient

Key Points

  • In the linear region: extension ∝ load (Hooke's Law obeyed)
  • Spring constant k = gradient of linear section = F/x
  • Beyond elastic limit: spring is permanently deformed; Hooke's Law no longer applies
  • Applies to both extension AND compression (Paper 4 Q10)

Paper 4 Q10 — Car Springs

4 springs; total weight = 17,000 N; force per spring = 17,000 ÷ 4 = 4,250 N
k = 2500 N/cm; x = F/k = 4250/2500 = 1.7 cm compression
Original length = 24 + 1.7 = 25.7 cm

Paper 2 Q33 — Reading from graph

Spring unloaded length = 4.0 cm; loaded length = 5.0 cm → extension = 1.0 cm. From graph at x=1.0 cm, load = 2.0 N → Answer B

Paper 2 Q40 — Spring constant from curved graph

Graph is not linear → Hooke's Law not obeyed for whole range. Read from the linear section only: gradient = F/x in the linear region.

  • Object sinks if its density > density of liquid

PR2 Graph Skills
▼

Plotting a Graph

  1. Label both axes with quantity and unit (e.g. "time / min")
  2. Choose a scale that uses more than half the grid
  3. Plot each point accurately with a small × or dot
  4. Draw a best-fit line (straight or smooth curve) — equal scatter either side
  5. Don't connect dot-to-dot zigzag lines

Best-fit Line vs Curve

  • Straight line: for directly proportional relationships (e.g. solubility vs volume)
  • Smooth curve: for non-linear relationships (e.g. enzyme rate vs temperature, foam height vs time)
  • Anomalous points: ignore when drawing best-fit, but don't delete from table

Reading from Graphs

  • Interpolation: reading within the data range (reliable)
  • Extrapolation: reading beyond the data range (less reliable)
  • Always show construction lines on the graph when asked to "determine" a value

Describing Relationships

  • Linear: "As X increases, Y increases proportionally" or "Y is directly proportional to X"
  • Curve levelling off: "Rate of increase decreases" or "Y increases at a decreasing rate"
  • Foam height (Paper 6 Q1): increases rapidly at first, then levels off as substrate is used up

Why Repeat and Average?

Repeating the experiment improves reliability. Taking the average reduces the effect of anomalous/random results, making the conclusion more valid.

Safety Points — Paper 6 Specific

  • Delivery tube + limewater: always remove delivery tube from limewater BEFORE stopping heating → prevents suck-back (limewater drawn back into hot tube → thermal shock → tube cracks)
  • Hard-glass test-tube: used for heating → withstands higher temperatures without cracking
  • Hot water experiment: use heat-resistant mat, care with boiling water, use thermometer with appropriate range

Suggesting Improvements

  • Use a balance instead of spatula loads → more precise mass measurement
  • Use a finer powder → dissolves faster / more accurately
  • Use a water bath to control temperature
  • Take more readings at shorter intervals
  • Use a gas syringe instead of foam height → more precise volume measurement

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Saying enzyme is "destroyed" or "killed" — always say denatured
  • Forgetting units in calculation answers
  • Confusing mass (kg, constant everywhere) and weight (N, varies with gravity)
  • Not explaining WHY in "explain" questions — describing what happens is not enough
  • Confusing diffusion and osmosis — osmosis is specifically water through a partially permeable membrane
  • Saying active transport doesn't need energy — it DOES require ATP
  • Drawing graph lines that connect every dot (zigzag) — draw a smooth best-fit instead
  • Not labelling graph axes with both quantity and unit
  • Confusing Benedict's (reducing sugars) with biuret (proteins) — Benedict's goes orange-red, biuret goes purple
  • Not showing working in calculations — no working = no method marks if final answer wrong
  • Ignoring the scale on a diagram — e.g. pendulum diagram 1:5 means actual = measured × 5
